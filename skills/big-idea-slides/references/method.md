# Method - finding the three insights and testing the idea

Condensed from the `big-idea` skill (the analysis method) so this skill is self-contained.
Where both are installed, `big-idea` produces the one-page sheet; this skill turns it into
slides. Section names below match the spec fields the builder expects.

```
CONSUMER PULSE (comments, reviews)        ->  CONSUMER INSIGHT   (one line)  relevance
COMPETITORS' OWNED CHANNELS / ADS         ->  CATEGORY INSIGHT   (one line)  distinctiveness
CLIENT DOCUMENTS (brief, tracker, report) ->  BRAND INSIGHT      (one line)  ownership
                                   |
                              THE BIG IDEA
                                   |
                  strategy statement -> comms platform -> campaign concept
```

An idea built on one insight is a tactic. On two, it is either unownable (no brand insight),
irrelevant (no consumer insight), or me-too (no category insight).

## What an insight is

A tension: something true, slightly uncomfortable, and not yet said by the brand or the
category. Tests for every line:

1. **One sentence.** No semicolon holding two ideas together.
2. **Tension.** It contains a "but", or implies one - a want against a barrier.
3. **Evidenced.** You can point to the verbatims, posts or document pages behind it.
4. **Not a restated brief.** The client would not have written it themselves.
5. **Useful.** It suggests what communication must do, without yet saying how.

Write three to five candidates per insight and choose. The first line written is usually a
summary, not an insight. Keep the rejected candidates - they are useful when a client
challenges the chosen one.

## Per insight

**Consumer.** Read for register, not topic counts: frequency tells you where to look, the
language people use tells you what kind of problem it is. Look for the gap between what they
want and what they expect to get. Note the corpus limits - a self-selected corpus is not a
satisfaction measure, and a corpus filtered to the client is not the category.

**Category.** Identify the convention (what nearly every brand says, shows and proves), then
the silence (what the consumer insight shows matters that nobody addresses). Ask **why** the
category behaves that way - the mechanism is often the insight itself. Before claiming a space
is empty, check it is not empty because it does not work.

**Brand.** Find what the brand has that rivals cannot copy, and what it faces (the weak
measure, the funnel stage where people drop out). Test the client's self-belief against the
consumer evidence; where they disagree, that contradiction is usually the brand insight. If a
document is referenced but was not supplied, mark the insight provisional and say what is
needed to confirm it.

**Channel (optional).** How people use media in this decision. It does not change the idea; it
shapes the media principles on the last slide.

## Testing the idea

- **Remove one insight** - the idea should fail. If it survives without the consumer insight it
  is not relevant; without the category insight, not distinctive; without the brand insight,
  not ownable.
- **Swap in a competitor's name.** If it still works, it is not the brand's idea. Name which
  part a specific competitor could take, and lead with the part they cannot.
- **Single-minded.** One thought. "Trusted, modern and fair" is three.
- **Credible today, or with a stated commitment.** If the idea needs the client to change
  something, say so - that is the price of the idea, and clients reward being told it.
- **Organising.** Could a media planner, a social team and a film director each act on it
  without further explanation?
- **Travels.** It should work in a film, a bill insert, a reply to a comment, and in store.

## Where Big Idea sections fail

- An insight that is a finding restated ("PTCL has high awareness").
- An idea that is really a tagline. The idea is the thought; the line comes later.
- The brand insight copied from the brief - the brief is the client's hypothesis, not the
  brand truth.
- Averaging the three insights into something bland that offends none of them. The idea should
  resolve the tension, not dilute it.
- Invented evidence. Never fabricate a verbatim, statistic, competitor behaviour or tracker
  measure. If the evidence for a line is missing, say what would be needed to find it.
