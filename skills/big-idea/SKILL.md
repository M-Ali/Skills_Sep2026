# Big Idea

Research produces findings. Findings do not brief a creative team. A Big Idea does - one
organising thought that every piece of the plan works to, so the sum is greater than the
parts.

This skill turns three bodies of evidence into three single-line insights, and three
insights into one idea. The discipline is in the single lines: if an insight cannot be said
in one sentence, it has not been found yet.

## Where this sits

**Comm scan → Consumer insights → Brand laddering → The Big Idea.** This is stage 4, where the
earlier evidence converges: the consumer insight from stage 2, the category insight from stage
1, the brand insight from the client's own documents, with stage 3's ladder showing which level
the idea can credibly claim. For the deck section use `big-idea-slides`, then hand the idea to
`campaign-concept`.

## The method

```
CONSUMER PULSE (YouTube / social comments)      ->  CONSUMER INSIGHT   (one line)
COMPETITORS' SOCIAL MEDIA PAGES                 ->  CATEGORY INSIGHT   (one line)
CLIENT DOCUMENTS (pitch brief, BHT, research)   ->  BRAND INSIGHT      (one line)
                                                          |
                                                          v
                                                     THE BIG IDEA
                                                          |
                                         strategy statement -> comms platform
                                                          |
                                           creative strategy (campaign-concept)
```

Each insight answers a different question, and the idea must answer all three:
- **Consumer** - what is true for the people we need, that they feel but the category ignores? (relevance)
- **Category** - what is everyone else doing, and what does that leave open? (distinctiveness)
- **Brand** - what does this brand have, believe or face that makes the open space credibly its own? (ownership)

An idea built on one insight is a tactic. On two, it is either unownable (no brand insight),
irrelevant (no consumer insight), or me-too (no category insight).

## What an insight is - and is not

An insight is a **tension**: something true, slightly uncomfortable, and not yet said by
the brand or category. A fact is not an insight.

- Fact: "Customers complain about rising bills."
- Insight: "Customers don't mind paying more; they mind finding out after they have committed."

Tests for every single line:
1. **One sentence**, no semicolons holding two ideas together.
2. **Tension** - it contains a "but", or implies one (a want against a barrier, a belief
   against a behaviour).
3. **Evidenced** - you can point to the verbatims, posts or document pages behind it.
4. **Not a restated brief** - the client would not have written it themselves.
5. **Useful** - it suggests what communication must do, without yet saying how.

Write 3-5 candidates for each insight, then choose. The first line written is usually a
summary, not an insight.

## Step 1 - Consumer Insight (from the Consumer Pulse)

Source: YouTube / social comments. If they have not been analysed, run the `comment-analysis`
skill first; this step starts from its pulse, pain points, "what they seek" and verbatims.

- Read for **register**, not topic counts. Frequency ranks where to look; the language
  people use ("fraud", "begging", "no option") is what tells you the kind of problem.
- Look for the gap between **what they want** and **what they believe they will get**.
- Distil to one line in the consumer's frame, not the brand's.
- Keep 2-3 verbatims beside it as proof. Note the corpus limits (self-selected, not a
  satisfaction measure).

## Step 2 - Category Insight (from competitors' social media pages)

Source: competitors' Instagram / Facebook / YouTube / TikTok pages. If they have not been
coded, run `category-creative-scan` (or `competitor-comms-audit` for paid advertising) first.

- Identify the **category convention**: what nearly every brand says, shows and proves.
- Identify the **category silence**: what the consumer insight shows matters, that no
  competitor addresses (objections answered, proof devices, territories left vacant).
- Ask **why** the category behaves this way (structure, footprint, regulation, habit) - the
  mechanism often is the insight.
- Distil to one line: the convention and what it leaves unsaid. Cite counts (n of N posts).

Before claiming a space is empty, check it is not empty because it does not work.

## Step 3 - Brand Insight (from client documents)

Source: what the client supplies - pitch brief, RFP/RFQ, BHT (brand health tracker), usage &
attitude studies, annual report, past campaigns. Run `brief-to-plan` for a brief, and
`consumer-brand-review` or `public-sector-review` for background, if not already done.

- Find what the brand **has** that competitors cannot copy (scale, reach, heritage, mandate,
  ownership, a number only it can publish).
- Find what the brand **faces**: the BHT's weak measures, the funnel stage where people drop
  out (awareness high, consideration low?), the stated business problem.
- **Test the client's self-belief against the Consumer Pulse.** Where the brief says one
  thing and the comments say another, that contradiction is frequently the brand insight -
  and the most valuable thing in the pitch. Say it diplomatically, but say it.
- Distil to one line about the brand's truth, not its ambition.
- If a document is referenced but was not supplied (e.g. the BHT), say so and mark the
  brand insight as provisional.

## Step 4 - The Big Idea

Place the three lines side by side and look for the one thought that resolves all of them:
the consumer tension, answered in the space the category leaves open, by something only
this brand can credibly do.

Write it three ways, as in the source framework:
1. **The Big Idea** - the organising thought, in plain words (not a tagline).
2. **Strategy statement** - how the brand will use the insights to meet the objective:
   *"Re-frame [brand] from [current perception] to [desired role] by [how]."*
3. **Comms platform** - one simple, unifying statement that guides every channel:
   *"Position [brand] as the [ROLE / CHAMPION OF ...]."*

Tests the idea must pass:
- **Remove one insight - does the idea still hold?** It should not. If it survives without
  the consumer insight, it is not relevant; without the category insight, not distinctive;
  without the brand insight, not ownable.
- **Swap in a competitor's name.** If it still works, it is not the brand's idea.
- **Single-minded.** One thought. "Trusted, modern and fair" is three ideas.
- **Credible today, or with a stated commitment.** If the idea requires the client to change
  something (pricing terms, service standards), say so explicitly - clients reward being
  told the price of an idea.
- **Organising.** Could a media planner, a social team and a film director each act on it
  without further explanation?
- **Travels.** It should work in a film, a bill insert, a reply to a comment and a store.

Offer 2-3 candidate Big Ideas when the evidence genuinely supports more than one, each with
its trade-off, then recommend one.

## Optional - Channel Insight

The source framework (Strategy and the Big Idea) also uses a **channel insight**: how people
use media and information in this decision (e.g. "Dr Google" reconfirms and confuses). When
available - for example, the comments show purchase decisions being made in YouTube threads -
record it as one line. It does not change the Big Idea; it shapes the **media principles**
that direct activation.

## Output

Produce a one-page Big Idea sheet, in this order:

```
CONSUMER INSIGHT   one line
                   evidence: 2-3 verbatims, source, corpus size and limits
CATEGORY INSIGHT   one line
                   evidence: counts from the scan (n of N posts), brands named
BRAND INSIGHT      one line
                   evidence: document and page / BHT measure; flag if provisional
(CHANNEL INSIGHT   one line, if available)

THE BIG IDEA       one organising thought
STRATEGY           Re-frame [brand] from ... to ... by ...
COMMS PLATFORM     Position [brand] as the ...
WHY IT HOLDS       one line each: relevant / distinctive / ownable
WHAT IT ASKS       the commitments the client must make for the idea to be true
MEDIA PRINCIPLES   3-5, from the channel insight (if available)
NEXT               hand to campaign-concept for proposition, line and executions
```

For a deck: one slide per insight (line large, evidence small beneath), then one slide
where the three lines converge into the Big Idea, then strategy statement and comms platform.

## Where Big Ideas fail

- **An insight that is a finding restated.** "PTCL has high awareness" is a data point.
- **An idea that is a tagline.** The Big Idea is the thought; the line comes later.
- **The brand insight copied from the brief.** The brief is the client's hypothesis, not
  the brand truth. Check it against the consumer.
- **Averaging the three insights** into something bland that offends none of them. The
  idea should resolve the tension, not dilute it.
- **Evidence-free lines.** Never invent a verbatim, statistic, competitor behaviour or BHT
  measure. If the evidence for an insight is missing, say what is needed to find it.
